Summary
Very dangerous avalanche conditions. Natural avalanches are likely. Travel in avalanche terrain not recommended.
  • Continue to make conservative terrain choices while the storm snow settles and stabilizes.
  • Keep your guard up at lower elevations. Storms slabs have been reactive at all elevations.

Further Detail
Avalanche Summary

On Tuesday, a widespread cycle of natural, human, and explosive triggered storm slab avalanches up to size 2 occurred in the Lizard Range. We are aware of an emerging avalanche incident in the Coal Creek area southwest of Fernie. Further details are not yet available, but will be provided when possible.

Snowpack Summary

Roughly 50 cm of storm snow has developed touchy storm slabs over an upper snowpack that is variable and contains two distinct layers:

  • At 50 cm depth, a supportive crust exists on south-facing terrain. On north-facing terrain, this crust is breakable or absent.

  • Between 60 and 100 cm depth, a persistent weak layer of crust/facets with spotty surface hoar is present.

The persistent weak layer is becoming less reactive but continues to produce sporadic avalanches, particularly on north aspects where a supportive crust is absent and in less-travelled areas like the Flathead. Recent storm snow will further load these layers.

Lakeman’s Lookout

The following snippet is taken from the Mountain Forecast section of FAR’s website. Bear in mind that it will be up to 24 hours old by the time you read it! Many thanks as always to Ron Lakeman for his hard work.

Mountain Weather Report


ISSUED: Tuesday February 17, 2025 11:00 MST

SYNOPSIS: A southerly feed of Pacific moisture originating from an upper low pressure centred off the south coast of BC-coastal Washington will provide further snow, possibly heavy at times, this morning and early afternoon. The snow should ease to a few flurries later this afternoon. Partial clearing is possible tonight. A broad upper trough of low pressure should provide variable cloud Wednesday and an increased chance of flurries Wednesday afternoon & Wednesday night. Meanwhile a modified Arctic airmass should gradually push west of Alberta for increased wind and colder trending temperatures beginning late today or tonight.

FREEZING LEVEL: Surface.

FERNIE ALPINE RESORT FORECAST (1615 metres):

TODAY: Snow, possibly heavy at times during the morning and early afternoon, easing to a few flurries during the late afternoon. Accumulation from 12 to 18 cm. Increasing wind possible during the afternoon. High near -6, possibly trending colder during the afternoon.

TONIGHT: Cloudy with clear periods. 60% chance of early flurries, 40% chance of flurries overnight. Minor accumulation possible. Windy at times, possible blowing snow and a high wind chill. Low near -17.

WEDNESDAY: Cloudy with sunny periods. 40-60% chance of flurries, the greater chance during the mid and late afternoon. Minor accumulation possible. Windy at times, possible blowing snow and a high wind chill, mainly during the morning. High temperature near -14.

WEDNESDAY NIGHT: Cloudy with clear breaks and a 60% chance of flurries. Minor to low end light accumulation possible. Windy at times, high wind chill possible. Low -19 or -20.

OUTLOOK:

THURSDAY: Cloudy with sunny periods and slight chance of flurries. Possibly windy at times during the morning. High near -11. Low near -21.

FRIDAY: A mix of sun & cloud. High near -8. Low near -14.

SATURDAY: Sunny with cloudy periods, possible morning fog patches. Increasing wind. High near -5. Low near -9.

CONFIDENCE/DISCUSSION: Fair to good confidence of the general pattern, still poor to fair confidence of some local details. Tough call on the local amount of snow today, some guidance now favours the Purcells and the eastern side of the Rockies for the heavier amounts. The likelihood of low density snow and the possibility of Pacific moisture overrunning an advancing Arctic airmass further complicates the snowfall prediction. There’s also still some uncertainty of the timing/positioning of the Arctic airmass. Most of the guidance suggests it’ll arrive tonight, the associated surface based easterly flow could result in a greater than currently predicted chance of flurries tonight. Airmass instability should produce scattered flurries and is also a risk for local snow showers (greater rate of fall versus flurries) Wednesday afternoon and Wednesday night. Some guidance suggests an isolated or scattered coverage of flurries could redevelop Thursday. High pressure should dominate Thursday night through Saturday.

 

Ron Lakeman.
