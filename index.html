Further Detail
Avalanche Summary

On Friday numerous small storm slabs were observed in the region. Most of these avalanches occurred at treeline and below and failed on the late January surface hoar/crust layer described in the snowpack summary. They have been reported to have propagated widely.

Snowpack Summary

By Saturday morning 10 to 30 cm of snow could overlie the late January surface hoar/crust layer. This new snow has been accompanied by strong south and west wind, meaning that the crust is still on the surface on southerly aspects and deeper deposits will be found on north and east aspects The late January surface hoar is largest on sheltered treeline and below treeline features and may not exist on exposed terrain in the alpine. The mid and lower snowpack is well settled.

Mountain Weather Report


ISSUED: Friday January 30, 2025 11:00 MST (update)

SYNOPSIS: Periods of snow (rising snow level) and increased wind are expected as a Pacific system pushes eastward across southern BC today. An upper trough should follow the cold front for a few snow showers and gusty winds during the late afternoon and evening, the snow should ease off later tonight. High pressure should briefly build over southern BC for dry, fairly sunny to partly cloudy conditions Saturday. The leading edge of another Pacific system should clip southeastern BC with increased cloud and a slight chance of flurries Saturday night.

FREEZING LEVEL: Rising to between 1600 & 1800 metres this afternoon. Dropping to between 1100 & 1400 metres overnight. Rising to between 1700 & 1900 metres Saturday afternoon.

FERNIE ALPINE RESORT FORECAST (1615 metres):

TODAY: Periods of snow, possible late day snow showers. Snow level rising to near 1600 metres. Accumulation from 8 to 15 cm. Increasing wind. High 0 or 1.

TONIGHT: A few snow showers with gusty winds and then a few flurries, easing off overnight. Accumulation from 4 to 8 cm. Possible clear periods and fog patches developing late overnight. Low -1 or -2.

SATURDAY: Possible morning low cloud and fog patches, otherwise sunny with afternoon cloudy periods. High 2 or 3.

SATURDAY NIGHT: Variable cloud and a 40% chance of flurries during the evening. Partial clearing overnight. Possibly windy at times. Low near -3.

OUTLOOK:

SUNDAY: A mix of sun & cloud with possible fog patches during the morning, increasing cloud during the afternoon. High 1 or 2. A few flurries or occasional snow Sunday night, light accumulation possible. Low near -2.

MONDAY: Cloudy with possible sunny breaks and morning fog patches. High 0 or 1. A few flurries or occasional snow Monday night, light accumulation possible. Low near -1.

TUESDAY: Cloudy with sunny periods, possible fog patches and a slight chance of flurries during the morning. High near 2. Low near -2.

CONFIDENCE/DISCUSSION: Fair confidence. The current system accelerated eastward overnight. The most recent guidance is increasingly variable with the local amount of snow today and tonight, partly due to the potential for variable snow showers during the late afternoon and evening. There’s also some uncertainty of the snow level this afternoon, it could briefly rise above 1600 metres. Fernie should reside between fronts Sunday. The computer models are increasingly inconsistent with the timing and moisture content of two Pacific systems Sunday night through Monday night or early Tuesday. There’s also some uncertainty of the snow level again at times Sunday night through Monday night. A strong upper ridge of high pressure could dominate Tuesday afternoon onward.
