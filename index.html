Snowpack Summary

Up to 4 mm of precipitation is in the forecast for Wednesday, and may fall as rain or snow at treeline. Widespread wind effect at upper elevations left a variety of pressed surfaces, slabs, and sastrugi in open terrain. Solar-facing slopes and lower elevations likely have a surface crust. Multiple weak layers of surface hoar and/or crusts/facets may exist in the upper meter of the snowpack; the most reactive is from late January, buried 40 to 90 cm deep. Most recent persistent slab avalanches failed on this surface hoar layer in sheltered areas at treeline and below. This layer has been less reactive recently near the ski hill, but riders continue to trigger it sporadically throughout the forecast region.

Mountain Weather Report


ISSUED: Wednesday March 4, 2026 11:00 MST

SYNOPSIS: A weakening Pacific frontal system (cold front) and following upper trough of low pressure will push eastward across southeastern BC for occasional snow/rain this morning and then a few flurries, possible snow showers and gusty winds this afternoon and evening. Limited flurries could linger overnight. Patchy moisture entrained in a developing northwesterly flow aloft suggests mainly cloudy skies to variable cloud cover and a 40% chance of flurries at times Thursday and Thursday night. 

FREEZING LEVEL: Between 1700 & 1900 metres this morning, fluctuating between 1500 & 1800 metres this afternoon. Dropping to or near surface tonight. Rising to between 1500 & 1700 metres Thursday afternoon. 

FERNIE ALPINE RESORT FORECAST (1615 metres):

TODAY: Occasional rain/wet snow during the morning, a few flurries and possible snow showers during the afternoon. Snow level near 1600-1700 metres, trending slightly lower during the afternoon. Accumulation from 1 to 4 cm. Windy at times. High 1 or 2.

TONIGHT: Cloudy with a few flurries, gusty winds and possible snow showers during the evening. Diminishing chance of flurries and possible clear breaks developing overnight. Light accumulation, possible 3 to 8 cm. Low -2 or -3.

THURSDAY: Cloudy with a few sunny breaks. 40% chance of flurries, mainly during the mid-late afternoon. Gusty afternoon winds. High near 1.

THURSDAY NIGHT: Cloudy with clear breaks, slight chance of evening flurries. Low -4 or -5.

OUTLOOK:

FRIDAY: Cloudy with sunny breaks. Possibly windy at times. High 2 or 3. 40% chance of flurries late Friday night. Low near 0.

SATURDAY: Mainly cloudy. 40% chance of light rain or wet flurries. Windy. High near 4. Low 0 or 1.

SUNDAY: Cloudy with a 70% chance of rain or wet snow. Windy. High 2 or 3. 60% chance of snow Sunday evening. Low -1 or -2.

CONFIDENCE/DISCUSSION: Fair to good confidence of the synoptic pattern through Thursday night, poor to fair Friday onward. Also still poor to fair confidence of the snow level and the local amount of snow today and tonight. The current disturbance has weakened and some guidance is tending much lighter with the local amount of precipitation during its passage this morning and early afternoon. Airmass instability should produce variable (possibly heavy in some areas) snow showers during the late afternoon and evening however the computer models are increasingly variable with where these cells will develop. The recent trend now suggests they could be confined just south of Fernie. A mild southwesterly flow should persist this morning, the snow level should trend lower after the cold fronts passage mid or late afternoon. Airmass instability could result in a greater than currently predicted chance of flurries Thursday afternoon and evening. A series of Pacific systems should drift over upper ridge of high pressure centred off the south coast and largely dissipate before they reach the Rockies Friday and Saturday. The computer models are highly variable with the chance of local precipitation and type of precipitation Saturday and Saturday night. A more significant wave of Pacific moisture is possible Sunday and Sunday night.

 

Ron Lakeman.
