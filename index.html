Avalanche Bulletin

Updated on 2026-02-14 17:00:00 MST, valid until 2026-02-15 17:00:00 MST.

Valid for: Flathead-Lizard.

Summary
Don't underestimate what the February sun can do to fresh snow. Storm slabs will likely be reactive on Sunday.
  • Don't let the desire for deep powder pull you into high consequence terrain.
  • Continue to make conservative terrain choices while the storm snow settles and stabilizes.
  • Avoid sun-exposed slopes, especially if the snow surface is moist or wet.

Further Detail
Avalanche Summary

Widespread storm slab avalanche activity was observed on Saturday. Slabs were size 1.5 to 2, triggered naturally and artificially.

Snowpack Summary

30 to 40 cm of new snow sits over a variety of surfaces including wind affected snow, surface hoar in sheltered areas and crust on solar aspects and low elevations. Below this, depending on aspect and elevation, the upper snowpack may contain two more layers of crust, facets and/or surface hoar formed in late January and early February. We have uncertainty around the reactivity of these layers. The mid and lower snowpack remain well settled, with no significant concerns at this time.

Lakeman’s Lookout

The following snippet is taken from the Mountain Forecast section of FAR’s website. Bear in mind that it will be up to 24 hours old by the time you read it! Many thanks as always to Ron Lakeman for his hard work.

Mountain Weather Report


ISSUED: Saturday February 14, 2025 11:15 MST

SYNOPSIS: A band of Pacific moisture will continue eastward across the area with snow showers this morning which should ease to scattered flurries this afternoon. A west-southwesterly flow of patchy moisture will persist for mainly cloudy skies or variable cloud cover and a 70% chance of flurries tonight. There’s still a slight chance of flurries early Sunday morning, otherwise Fernie should briefly reside between disturbances for partly cloudy skies Sunday. Increasing cloud is expected Sunday night and light snow could develop by Monday morning as a significant surge of moisture streams north-northeastward from a deepening trough of low pressure off the coast of Washington-Oregon. 

FREEZING LEVEL: Rising to between 1300 & 1500 metres this afternoon. Surface tonight. Rising to between 1200 & 1400 metres Sunday afternoon.

FERNIE ALPINE RESORT FORECAST (1615 metres):

TODAY: Morning snow showers, mainly cloudy with a few flurries during the afternoon. Accumulation from 6 to 12 cm. Possibly windy at times. High near -1.

TONIGHT: Cloudy with clear breaks and a 70% chance of flurries. Accumulation from a trace to 4 cm. Windy at times. Low -5 or -6.

SUNDAY: Cloudy with sunny periods, slight chance of early morning flurries. Windy at times. High near -2.

SUNDAY NIGHT: Clear with cloudy periods during the evening, increasing cloud overnight. 40% chance of flurries by morning. Low near -7.

OUTLOOK:

MONDAY and MONDAY NIGHT: Periods of snow, possibly heavy at times. Moderate to heavy accumulation possible. Increasing wind during the night. High near -3. Low near -7.

TUESDAY: Mainly cloudy with a few flurries, minor to light accumulation possible. Windy. High near -7, possibly trending colder during the afternoon. Low near -15.

WEDNESDAY: Mainly cloudy with a few flurries, minor to light accumulation possible. Possibly windy at times. High near -9. Low near -17.

CONFIDENCE/DISCUSSION: Fair confidence through Sunday night, poor to fair Monday onward. Airmass instability produced locally heavy snow showers overnight which appear to have eased to flurries during the past couple of hours. Isolated snow showers could redevelop later this morning or early this afternoon. Another disturbance should drift northeastward from Washington-Idaho tonight, the majority of its snow is currently suggested to remain just south of Fernie. Some guidance suggests greater than currently predicted clearing Sunday. The computer models are highly variable with the timing/positioning of a substantial wave of Pacific moisture which could produce a moderate to heavy amount of snow. Earlier runs favoured Monday night and Tuesday for the heavier snow, the most recent solutions now favour Monday afternoon and evening, more changes are likely. A large upper trough of low pressure should dominate Tuesday and Wednesday. Meanwhile a modified Arctic airmass should gradually push just west of Alberta beginning Tuesday or Tuesday night.

 

Ron Lakeman.
