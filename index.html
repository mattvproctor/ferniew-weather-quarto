Avalanche Bulletin

Updated on 2026-02-02 17:00:00 MST, valid until 2026-02-03 17:00:00 MST.

Valid for: Flathead-Lizard-Moyie-St. Mary.

Summary
We are uncertain how the buried persistent weak layer will react to the forecasted warming. In times of uncertainty, conservative terrain choices are our best defense.
  • The more the snowpack warms up and weakens, the more conservative your terrain selection should be.
  • Remote triggering is a concern; avoid terrain where triggering overhead slopes is possible.
  • Avoid terrain traps such as gullies and cliffs where the consequence of any avalanche could be serious.
  • Back off slopes as the surface becomes moist or wet with rising temperatures.

Problems
Problem 1: Storm slab.

New snow continues to build storm slabs, which are expected to remain reactive due to the underlying layer of large surface hoar and a crust. Be aware of remote triggering and wide propagation under these conditions.



Further Detail
Avalanche Summary

On Sunday, wet loose avalanches were observed on steep terrain at lower elevations, while riders were able to trigger storm slabs (size 1.5) on easterly features at treeline. Evidence of a natural cycle from Saturday night is still visible in the Lizard range with numerous storm slabs (size 1.5 to 2) released on north to east alpine and treeline slopes. All of these avalanches failed on the late January surface hoar/crust layer described in the snowpack summary.

Snowpack Summary

10 to 15 cm of new snow now buries old surfaces, including a sun crust on south to east aspects, wind slabs in open areas and settling storm snow on north to east aspects. Moderate to strong south and west wind have affected the snow distribution, creating deeper deposits on lee slopes. The late January surface hoar/crust layer is 20 to 40 cm deep and has shown signs of recent reactivity in the past 4 days, especially in sheltered treeline and below treeline features. At 1600 meters and below, the snow surface is moist due to daytime warming. The mid and lower snowpack is well settled.

Mountain Weather Report


ISSUED: Monday February 2, 2025 11:00 MST

SYNOPSIS: A weak Pacific disturbance will push eastward across southern BC for increasing cloud this morning and occasional light precipitation later this afternoon and this evening. A strong upper ridge of high pressure should build northward from California-Oregon for limited or partial clearing overnight and dry, uneventful conditions Tuesday onward. Unseasonably mild temperatures will continue.

FREEZING LEVEL: Rising to between 1600 & 1800 metres this afternoon. A freezing or near freezing layer should redevelop at surface tonight. Rising to between 1800 & 2100 metres Tuesday afternoon.

FERNIE ALPINE RESORT FORECAST (1615 metres):

TODAY: A few early-mid morning sunny breaks, otherwise cloudy. 70% chance of light snow/rain developing mid afternoon. Snow level 1500-1700 metres, minor accumulation possible. Gusty afternoon winds. High 1 or 2.

TONIGHT: Occasional (generally light) snow/rain during the evening, minor accumulation of snow possible. Cloudy with possible clear periods and fog patches overnight. Low near -1.

TUESDAY: Possible low cloud and fog patches, otherwise a mix of sun & cloud. High 2 or 3.

TUESDAY NIGHT: Cloudy with clear periods and fog patches. Low 0 or -1.

OUTLOOK:

WEDNESDAY: Cloudy with fog patches, possible sunny afternoon sunny periods. High 3 or 4. Low near 0.

THURSDAY: Cloudy with fog patches, possible sunny periods. High 3 or 4. Low 0 or -1.

FRIDAY: Cloudy with fog patches, possible sunny periods. High 1 or 2. Low near -2.

CONFIDENCE/DISCUSSION: Good confidence of the synoptic pattern through Thursday, fair beyond. Poor to fair confidence of some local details. Most of the guidance suggests a light amount of precipitation this afternoon and evening, favouring the 2 PM through 8 PM period. The snow level is highly questionable. All of the computer models are in agreement for a strong upper ridge of high pressure to dominate Tuesday through Friday. Patchy moisture should drift over the area of high pressure for cloudy periods or variable cloud at times Tuesday afternoon through Wednesday morning. Low cloud and fog patches are also expected tonight onward with a deepening temperature inversion and stagnating low level airmass. As is common with high pressure during the winter months there’s much uncertainty of when and where the low cloud/fog will develop, how deep it’ll be and when & where it’ll dissipate. The longer range computer models suggest a Pacific system is possible Sunday-Sunday night.

 

Ron Lakeman.
