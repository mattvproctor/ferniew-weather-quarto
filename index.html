Avalanche Bulletin

Updated on 2026-02-05 17:00:00 MST, valid until 2026-02-06 17:00:00 MST.

Valid for: Flathead-Lizard-Moyie-St. Mary.

Summary
Warm temperatures continue to form cohesive slabs over a widespread weak layer. The likelihood and potential size of slab avalanches largely depend on the depth of this layer.
  • The more the snowpack warms up and weakens, the more conservative your terrain selection should be.
  • Avoid areas where the snow feels stiff and/or slabby.
  • Remote triggering is a concern; avoid terrain where triggering overhead slopes is possible.
  • Avoid travelling on slopes below cornices.

Further Detail
Avalanche Summary

A natural avalanche cycle occurred last weekend with numerous slab avalanches up to size 2. All of these appeared to fail on the late-January surface hoar/crust/facet layer outlined in the snowpack summary. While avalanche activity has largely decreased since last weekend, several small (size 1 to 1.5) human-triggered avalanches have occurred throughout the week on a variety of aspects at treeline and above.

Snowpack Summary

Either a thin surface crust or moist snow is expected Friday morning, depending on the strength of the overnight freeze. Continued above-freezing temperatures and sunny skies will likely create moist snow surfaces on all aspects and at all elevations by Friday afternoon. Approximately 10 to 30 cm of snow continues to settle into cohesive slabs over the late-January weak layer. This layer consists of a melt-freeze crust of variable thickness, with a possibility of surface hoar on top and faceted snow above and/or below the crust. The mid and lower snowpack is generally well settled, with no significant concerns.

Mountain Weather Report


ISSUED: Thursday February 5, 2025 11:00 MST

SYNOPSIS: An upper ridge of high pressure will maintain dry, fairly sunny/clear and unseasonably mild conditions today and Friday. A deep temperature inversion and stagnating airmass could also result in some low cloud and fog at times tonight, Friday morning and again Friday night. 

FREEZING LEVEL: Rising to between 2600 & 3000 metres today, beginning to gradually trend lower Friday. A freezing or near freezing layer should redevelop at surface tonight.

FERNIE ALPINE RESORT FORECAST (1615 metres):

TODAY: Mainly sunny. Windy at times, mainly at the upper elevations and during the morning. High 7 or 8.

TONIGHT: Clear with a few bands of cloud. Low cloud and fog possible overnight. Low near -1.

FRIDAY: Possible low cloud and fog patches during the morning, otherwise sunny with a few bands of cloud. High 6 or 7.

FRIDAY NIGHT: Clear with cloudy periods, possible low cloud and fog patches. Low -1 or -2.

OUTLOOK:

SATURDAY: Possible morning low cloud and fog. Otherwise sunny during the morning and increasing cloud and increasing wind during the afternoon. High 2 or 3. Occasional rain/snow Saturday night, snow level 1500-1800 metres, light accumulation of snow possible. Low 0 or 1.

SUNDAY: Periods of snow/rain, possibly easing off during the afternoon. Snow level 1500-1800 metres. Light accumulation of snow possible. Windy at times. High 1 or 2. 60% chance of flurries or occasional snow Sunday night. Low -1 or -2.

MONDAY: Mainly cloudy with a 40% chance of flurries. High 0 or 1. Low near -3.

CONFIDENCE/DISCUSSION: Good confidence of the synoptic pattern through Friday night, fair Saturday and Saturday night, poor to fair Sunday onward. Also poor to fair confidence of the local sky condition and the local temperature tonight through Saturday morning. The low cloud and fog appear to be confined to the trench this morning. It’s difficult to accurately predict if low cloud/fog will develop locally tonight-early Friday and, or Friday night-early Saturday. The deep temperature inversion makes for some uncertainty of the local temperature this afternoon through Saturday. The most recent computer models have slowed the arrival of the next anticipated Pacific system slightly, now favouring Saturday mid evening through Sunday morning for its (questionable snow level) precipitation. The computer models are now also trending this system southeast of Fernie Sunday afternoon and confining precipitation associated with a following Pacific system just south of Fernie Sunday night and Monday. More changes are likely.
