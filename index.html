Further Detail
Avalanche Summary

A natural avalanche cycle occurred over the weekend with numerous slab avalanches up to size 2, primarily on north though east aspects at treeline and above. These all appeared to fail on the late January surface hoar/crust/facet layer outlined in the snowpack summary. Since the natural avalanche cycle, several small (size 1 to 1.5) human-triggered avalanches have been reported on a variety of aspects at treeline and above.

Snowpack Summary

Refrozen and crusty surfaces are likely on steep, south-facing slopes and across most below-treeline terrain on Wednesday morning. The upper snowpack is likely to become moist throughout the day, at all elevations, due to rapidly rising freezing levels. Approximately 20 to 50 cm of recent snow has buried a widespread melt–freeze crust, which may have surface hoar on top and faceted snow above and/or below. The mid- and lower snowpack is well-settled, with no significant concerns.

Mountain Weather Report


ISSUED: Tuesday February 3, 2025 11:00 MST

SYNOPSIS: Patchy moisture will drift over a strong upper ridge of high pressure (building northward from California-Oregon) for variable cloud cover today, tonight and early Wednesday. High pressure should be of greater influence for clearing or partial clearing Wednesday afternoon and evening. A deepening temperature inversion and trapped low level moisture will also allow for low cloud and fog patches at times during the next few days. The temperatures should trend milder.

FREEZING LEVEL: Rising to between 1700 & 2000 metres this afternoon. A freezing or near freezing layer should redevelop at surface tonight. Rising to between 1900 & 2200 metres Wednesday afternoon.

FERNIE ALPINE RESORT FORECAST (1615 metres):

TODAY: Cloudy with a few sunny breaks and fog patches. High 2 or 3.

TONIGHT: Cloudy with fog patches and possible clear breaks. Possibly windy at times. Low near -1.

WEDNESDAY: Cloudy with fog patches, sunny periods developing or becoming mainly sunny during the afternoon. Windy at times. High near 4.

WEDNESDAY NIGHT: Cloudy with clear periods and fog patches. Low 0 or -1.

OUTLOOK:

THURSDAY: Low cloud and fog patches, possibly dissipating to sunny periods or mainly sunny skies during the afternoon. High 4 or 5. Low near -2.

FRIDAY: Low cloud and fog patches, possible sunny periods. High 2 or 3. Low near -3.

SATURDAY: Possible low cloud and fog patches, otherwise increasing cloud and increasing wind. 40% chance of late day flurries/light rain. High near 2. 70% chance of occasional snow/rain Saturday night, light accumulation of snow possible. Low near 0.

CONFIDENCE/DISCUSSION: Good confidence of the synoptic pattern through Friday. Poor to fair beyond and also poor to fair confidence of some local details. A minor disturbance skirting north of Fernie is a small risk (20% chance) for flurries late tonight and early Wednesday. The timing of events is promising for clearing skies in its wake Wednesday afternoon. The stagnating low level airmass favours low cloud and fog patches developing Wednesday night but there’s always much uncertainty of when and where the low cloud/fog will develop, how deep it’ll be and then its persistence with high pressure during the winter months. High pressure is currently suggested to collapse Friday. A Pacific system could push inland Saturday and Saturday night. Its timing, moisture content and associated snow level are all highly questionable.

 

Ron Lakeman.
