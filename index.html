Valid for: Flathead-Lizard-Moyie-St. Mary.

Summary
A weak layer in the snowpack remains a concern for human-triggering. Don't let your guard down. Remain vigilance during travel and look for signs of instability like<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" href="https://avalanche.ca/glossary#whumpf"> whumpfing</a> and cracking.
  • Be careful with wind-loaded pockets, especially near ridge crests and rollovers.
  • Be alert to conditions that change with elevation, aspect, and exposure to wind.
  • Pay attention to isolated wind affected features in the alpine, as well as cross-loaded features at treeline.

Problems
Problem 1: Persistent slab.

A weak layer of surface hoar/crust/facets exists in the top 30 cm of the snowpack. It will be most sensitive to triggering in northerly upper elevation lees where the overlying snow is deepest and not capped by a more recent crust.



Further Detail
Avalanche Summary

A remote triggered small avalanche (size 1) was reported on Tuesday at treeline on a north facing slope. The South Rockies Field team reported widespread whumpfing as they travelled on north facing slopes in the alpine on Tuesday.

Snowpack Summary

Cool and clear conditions are promoting surface hoar growth, primarily in areas protected from the sun and wind. The upper snowpack consists of 10 to 15 cm of wind affected new snow that overlies a widespread variable crust on all but high alpine north facing terrain. A weak layer of surface hoar on a melt-freeze crust, with a thick layer of facets below, formed in late January is buried 20 to 50 cm deep. The mid and lower snowpack remain well settled, with no significant concerns at this time.

Mountain Weather Report


ISSUED: Thursday February 12, 2025 11:00 MST

SYNOPSIS: Limited moisture will drift over a flat area of high pressure for partly cloudy skies or variable cloud cover today, tonight and early Friday. A southwesterly feed of moisture in advance of a large upper trough digging southward off the coast of BC should result in increasing cloud by Friday afternoon and occasional snow or a few flurries Friday night.

FREEZING LEVEL: Rising to between 1400 & 1600 metres this afternoon. Surface tonight. Rising to between 1300 & 1500 metres Friday afternoon.

FERNIE ALPINE RESORT FORECAST (1615 metres):

TODAY: A mix of sun & cloud. High 0 or -1.

TONIGHT: Clear with cloudy periods during the evening, greater cloud cover and possible fog patches overnight. Low -4 or -5.

FRIDAY: Possible early morning sunny periods and fog patches, otherwise increasing cloud. Slight chance of late day flurries. Increasing wind during the afternoon. High -1 or -2.

FRIDAY NIGHT: Cloudy with a few flurries or occasional snow. Light accumulation, possible 3 to 8 cm. Windy at times. Low -4 or -5.

OUTLOOK:

SATURDAY and SATURDAY NIGHT: Cloudy with occasional snow or a few flurries, diminishing chance of flurries late overnight. Light accumulation. High near -1. Low near -7.

SUNDAY: Cloudy with sunny breaks or sunny periods. Slight chance of flurries, early morning and again late in the day. High near -2. 60% chance of flurries Sunday night. Low near -6.

MONDAY: Mainly cloudy with a 40% chance of flurries. High -3 or -4. Snow developing and increasing wind Monday night. Light accumulation possible. Low near -10.

CONFIDENCE/DISCUSSION: Fair to good confidence of the synoptic pattern through Saturday, fair Saturday night onward and also fair confidence of some local details during the shorter term. A stagnant low level airmass could still result in patchy low cloud/fog late tonight and Friday morning. The most recent computer models are in better agreement for occasional snow or at least a few flurries Friday night and Saturday. There’s still much uncertainty of how much snow will accumulate as the approaching disturbance should dissipate some before it reaches the Rockies. The guidance is increasingly variable with the chance of flurries Sunday night and then more significant snow developing Monday or Monday night. Some guidance suggests an Arctic outbreak beginning Monday night or Tuesday.

 

Ron Lakeman.
