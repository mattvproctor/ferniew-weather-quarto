Avalanche Bulletin

Updated on 2026-02-18 17:00:00 MST, valid until 2026-02-19 17:00:00 MST.

Valid for: Flathead-Lizard.

Summary
Storm snow has added load to a complex upper snowpack creating touchy avalanche conditions. Stick to lower angle terrain with no overhead hazard.
  • Continue to make conservative terrain choices while the storm snow settles and stabilizes.
  • Keep your guard up at lower elevations. Storms slabs have been reactive at all elevations.
  • Keep in mind that human triggering may persist as natural avalanches taper off.
  • Storm slabs in motion may step down to deeper layers resulting in large avalanches.

Further Detail
Avalanche Summary

There was an avalanche fatality in the Matheson Creek area on Tuesday. See this MIN report for more details. A cycle of natural, human, and explosive triggered storm slab avalanches up to size 2 occurred on Tuesday in the Lizard Range. In the Flathead range there were reports of several remotely triggered (from a distance of 100+ m) persistent slab avalanches. One of of these was a size 3 with a very wide propagation approximately 50-60 cm deep at 2000 m on a west aspect.

Snowpack Summary

Roughly 50 cm of storm snow has developed touchy storm slabs over an upper snowpack that is variable and contains two distinct layers:

  • At 50 cm depth, a supportive crust exists on south-facing terrain. On north-facing terrain, this crust is breakable or absent.

  • Between 60 and 100 cm depth, a persistent weak layer of crust/facets with spotty surface hoar is present.

The persistent weak layer is becoming less reactive but continues to produce sporadic avalanches, particularly on north aspects where a supportive crust is absent and in less-travelled areas like the Flathead. Recent storm snow has increased the load on these layers.

Mountain Weather Report


ISSUED: Thursday February 19, 2025 11:00 MST

SYNOPSIS: A large upper trough of low pressure will continue eastward across BC for variable cloud to mainly cloudy skies, a few flurries and possible snow showers today and tonight. Limited flurries could linger early Friday morning. Otherwise a relatively minor upper ridge of high pressure should shift eastward across southern BC for dry, partly cloudy conditions Friday and Friday night. A modified Arctic airmass will maintain cold temperatures today, a moderating trend should begin Friday.

FRIDAY NIGHT: Cloudy with clear periods. Low near -14.

OUTLOOK:

SATURDAY: A mix of sun & cloud, possible morning fog patches. Increasing wind. High near -5. Low near -10.

SUNDAY: Increasing cloud, slight chance of an afternoon flurry. Windy. High near -4. Occasional snow Sunday night, light accumulation possible. Low near -8.

MONDAY and MONDAY NIGHT: Periods of snow, moderate to heavy accumulation possible. Windy. High -3 or -4. Low -8 or -9.

CONFIDENCE/DISCUSSION: Fair to good confidence of the general pattern through Saturday night, fair Sunday onward. Poor to fair confidence of some local details (mainly the local amounts of snow) during the short term. An organized band of snow appears to have developed with the core of the upper trough over the West Kootenay this morning. This band of snow should continue eastward across the Fernie area late today and more so tonight. Airmass instability could result in highly variable snow showers and the temperature profile supports ultra low density snow which make for much uncertainty of the local accumulation of snow this afternoon and tonight. A dirty ridge of high pressure should dominate Friday afternoon through Saturday or Saturday night. A series of systems should push northeastward from the coast of Oregon-Washington late Sunday or Sunday night through Monday night. The timing and moisture content of these systems will likely require some adjustments. There’s also some uncertainty of the local temperature with the mild southwesterly flow aloft Saturday night onward and a surface based modified Arctic airmass centred just east of the Continental Divide also having some influence.
