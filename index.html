Problems
Problem 1: Persistent slab.

A weak layer of surface hoar on a melt freeze crust is buried on average less than 30 cm deep, but deeper pockets likely exist in wind-loaded areas. Both the likelihood of triggering and consequence are greatest in places where it is deepest.



Further Detail
Avalanche Summary

A natural avalanche cycle occurred last weekend with numerous slab avalanches up to size 2. All of these appeared to fail on the late-January surface hoar/crust/facet layer outlined in the snowpack summary. While avalanche activity has largely decreased since last weekend, several small (size 1 to 1.5) human-triggered avalanches have occurred throughout the week on a variety of aspects at treeline and above.

Snowpack Summary

Surfaces are a mix of refrozen crust and moist snow. 10 to 30 cm of snow sits over the late-January weak layer. This layer consists of a melt-freeze crust of variable thickness, with a possibility of surface hoar on top and faceted snow above and/or below the crust. This layer remains a concern at upper elevations that lack a robust crust on the surface. The mid and lower snowpack is generally well settled, with no significant concerns.

Mountain Weather Report


ISSUED: Friday February 6, 2025 11:00 MST

SYNOPSIS: High pressure will maintain dry, uneventful and unseasonably mild conditions as it gradually collapses southeastward during the next 24 hours. Patchy moisture entrained in a developing southwesterly flow aloft should result in cloudy periods today and a stagnant low level airmass could still result in some low cloud and fog tonight & early Saturday. A frontal system will track inland from the south coast for increasing cloud and increasing wind Saturday afternoon and then occasional precipitation Saturday night.

FREEZING LEVEL: Between 2500 & 3000 metres with a freezing layer at surface this morning, trending slightly lower this afternoon. A freezing or near freezing layer should redevelop at surface tonight. Between 1700 & 2000 metres Saturday.

FERNIE ALPINE RESORT FORECAST (1615 metres):

TODAY: Sunny with cloudy periods. High 5 or 6.

TONIGHT: Clear with cloudy periods. Low cloud and fog possible overnight. Low near -1.

SATURDAY: Sunny periods or low cloud and fog patches during the morning. Increasing cloud and increasing wind during the afternoon. 40% chance of light rain late in the day. High near 3.

SATURDAY NIGHT: Occasional rain/snow. Snow level 1500-1700 metres, light accumulation. Windy. Low 0 or 1.

OUTLOOK:

SUNDAY and SUNDAY NIGHT: Snow/rain during the early-mid morning, light accumulation of snow possible. Mainly cloudy with a 40% chance of flurries or light rain during the late morning and afternoon. Windy at times, mainly during the morning. High near 2. 70% chance of occasional snow (small risk of rain) redeveloping Sunday night, light accumulation possible. Low -1 or -2.

MONDAY: 60% chance of snow during the morning, minor to light accumulation possible. Cloudy with possible sunny breaks and a slight chance of flurries during the afternoon. High near 0. Low -3 or -4.

TUESDAY: Cloudy with sunny breaks and a slight chance of flurries. High near -1. Low near -4.

CONFIDENCE/DISCUSSION: Fair to good confidence of the synoptic pattern through Saturday night. Poor to fair Sunday onward and also poor to fair confidence of some finer details. The low cloud and fog appear to be confined to the trench again this morning, tough call if local low cloud/fog will develop tonight or early Saturday. A more extensive band of mid-upper level cloud is possible this afternoon and early evening. The deep temperature inversion maintains some uncertainty of the local temperature this afternoon through Saturday. The computer models are still in relatively good agreement with the Pacific system Saturday night, its main band of precipitation (questionable snow level) is now favoured for the late evening and overnight hours. This system should exit southeast of Fernie Sunday late morning-afternoon. The computer models are now trending precipitation (still a questionable but lower trending snow level) associated with a following system further north across Fernie late Sunday night and Monday morning, this could easily change again. A westerly feed of patchy moisture is currently expected Monday and Tuesday.
