Avalanche Bulletin

Updated on 2026-02-09 17:00:00 MST, valid until 2026-02-10 17:00:00 MST.

Valid for: Flathead-Lizard-Moyie-St. Mary.

Summary
Heightened avalanche conditions exist in specific terrain features where weak layers in the upper snowpack are possible to human-trigger. Continually assess conditions as you travel.
  • Be careful with wind-loaded pockets, especially near ridge crests and rollovers.
  • Keep in mind a buried crust offers an excellent bed surface for avalanches.
  • Be aware of the potential for large avalanches due to buried surface hoar.

Further Detail
Avalanche Summary

On Sunday, explosive control work in the alpine produced a few cornice results and several persistent slab avalanches size 1 to 1.5, failing on the January weak layer described in the snowpack summary. In the past week, human-triggered persistent slab avalanches on this layer were reported size 1 to 1.5, most occurring at treeline and above.

Snowpack Summary

Roughly 10 to 15 cm of wind affected new snow overlies a widespread variable crust on all but high alpine north facing terrain. A weak layer of surface hoar on a melt-freeze crust, with a thick layer of facets below, formed in late January is buried 20 to 50 cm deep. This well developed layer is expected to become increasingly problematic as they get buried deeper. The mid and lower snowpack remain well settled, with no significant concerns at this time.

Mountain Weather Report


ISSUED: Monday February 9, 2025 11:00 MST

SYNOPSIS: A series of weak disturbances/patchy moisture entrained in a westerly flow aloft will result in variable cloud cover to mainly cloudy skies and a 30-60% chance of flurries at times today through Tuesday evening. The greater chance of flurries tonight. A relatively minor area of high pressure should build inland from the south coast for clearing or partial clearing later Tuesday night.

FREEZING LEVEL: Rising to near 1500-1600 metres this afternoon. A freezing layer should redevelop at surface tonight. Rising to between 1300 & 1500 metres Tuesday afternoon.

FERNIE ALPINE RESORT FORECAST (1615 metres):

TODAY: Cloudy with sunny breaks and a slight chance of flurries. High near 0.

TONIGHT: Mainly cloudy with a 60% chance of flurries, minor accumulation possible. Low -3 or -4.

TUESDAY: Cloudy with a few sunny breaks and possible morning fog patches. 40% chance of flurries, mainly during the morning. High near -1.

TUESDAY NIGHT: Cloudy with clear breaks and a 30% chance of flurries during the evening. Clear with cloudy periods and possible fog patches overnight. Low near -7.

OUTLOOK:

WEDNESDAY: Sunny with cloudy periods, possible morning low cloud and fog patches. High near -2. Low near -7.

THURSDAY: Sunny with cloudy periods, possible morning low cloud and fog patches. High -1 or -2. Increasing cloud late Thursday night. Low -5 or -6.

FRIDAY: Possible morning sunny breaks and fog patches, otherwise mainly cloudy. 40% chance of late day flurries. High near -2. 60% chance of flurries or occasional snow Friday night. Low -5 or -6.

CONFIDENCE/DISCUSSION: Fair to good confidence on the general pattern through Thursday, fair beyond and also fair confidence of some local details during the short term. The computer models are rather variable with the moisture content of the westerly flow and the resulting chance of flurries today through Tuesday evening. Some solutions have increased the chance of a dusting of snow with a short wave trough timed for this evening. Otherwise local upslope conditions and airmass instability provide a slight chance of flurries each afternoon. A minor area of high pressure should dominate Wednesday and then flatten southward across southern BC Thursday. The computer models are increasingly variable with the timing and moisture content of a larger upper trough gradually pushing southeastward from the central coast Thursday night through Friday night. Some guidance downplays the increased cloud cover late Thursday night-Friday and the chance of snow Friday night.
