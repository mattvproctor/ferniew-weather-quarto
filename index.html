Further Detail
Avalanche Summary

On Tuesday, a naturally triggered size 2 wind slab and numerous explosive triggered size 2 wind slabs were reported on north and east aspects in the alpine. Numerous persistent slab avalanches up to size 3 were reported on all aspects and elevations over the weekend. They were triggered accidentally, naturally, and remotely. See recent MIN reports here and here for further details.

Snowpack Summary

Up to 20 cm of recent snow accompanied by strong southwest wind has formed deeper deposits on north and east aspects. In sheltered terrain it will overlie a layer of surface hoar or a sun crust. The snow surface is expected to become moist on sun exposed slopes. A couple concerning weak layers exist in the upper snowpack:

  • 60 to 80 cm deep, there is a supportive crust on south-facing terrain. On north-facing terrain, this layer maybe a breakable crust or surface hoar.

  • The late January layer is buried 70 to 120 cm deep. It consists of a crust with facets or surface hoar above it.

The mid and lower snowpack is well settled.

Mountain Weather Report


ISSUED: Wednesday February 25, 2026 11:15 MST

SYNOPSIS: A series of weak disturbances entrained in a strengthening west-northwesterly flow aloft will provide variable cloud cover or mainly cloudy skies this morning and then increased wind and a few flurries at times this afternoon, tonight and Thursday morning. A slightly more substantial upper trough should follow for occasional snow and significant wind Thursday afternoon and Thursday night.

FREEZING LEVEL: Rising to between 1300 & 1500 metres this afternoon and possibly a bit higher Thursday afternoon. At or slightly above surface tonight.

FERNIE ALPINE RESORT FORECAST (1615 metres):

TODAY: Cloudy with a few sunny breaks. 70% chance of mid-late afternoon flurries. Increasing wind, possibly becoming strong during the afternoon. High near -1.

TONIGHT: Mainly cloudy with a few flurries. Minor to low end light accumulation possible. Windy, strong at times. Low -1 or -2.

THURSDAY: Cloudy with a few flurries during the morning. Steadier snow developing during the afternoon. Light accumulation, possible 3 to 6 cm. Windy, strong at times, possible blowing snow. High 0 or -1.

THURSDAY NIGHT: Cloudy with occasional snow. Light accumulation, possible 4 to 8 cm. Windy, strong at times, possible blowing snow. Low -2 or -3.

OUTLOOK:

FRIDAY and FRIDAY NIGHT: Cloudy with a few flurries and then occasional snow, easing off overnight. Light accumulation. Windy at times. High -1 or -2. Low near -6.

SATURDAY: Sunny with cloudy periods. High near -2. Low -7 or -8.

SUNDAY: A mix of sun & cloud. High 0 or 1. Low near -4.

CONFIDENCE/DISCUSSION: Fair to good confidence of the synoptic pattern, fair confidence of many local details. A tightening pressure gradient should result in strong wind at times this afternoon through Friday. The computer models are still variable with the moisture content of the approaching disturbances, some solutions confine the main band of flurries just north and west of Fernie this afternoon and tonight. Thursday afternoon-evening and Friday-Friday evening still appear more promising for light accumulations of snow. There’s also some uncertainty of the local temperature this afternoon through the weekend. A colder airmass could attempt to push just west of Alberta but now appears more likely to be confined east of Fernie Friday night and Saturday. A relatively dry westerly flow is currently expected Saturday and Sunday.
