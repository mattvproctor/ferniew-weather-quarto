Further Detail
Avalanche Summary

On Sunday, explosives triggered several persistent slab avalanches, many at treeline with 40 to 70 cm crowns. On Saturday, skiers remotely triggered a size 1.5 avalanche from 30 m away while regrouping; it failed on surface hoar with a 55 cm crown depth. On Friday, riders encountered extreme winds, whumpfing, and shooting cracks extending up to 200 m into lower-angle terrain. Snowpack tests found surface hoar buried 40-60 cm, with easy and reactive results.

Snowpack Summary

Widespread wind effect left a variety of pressed surfaces, slabs, and sastrugi in open terrain. Solar slopes have a crust and may turn moist during the day. There are multiple weak layers of surface hoar and/or crusts/facets found in the upper 100 cm of the snowpack; the most reactive is 40 to 60 cm deep. The majority of recent persistent slab avalanches have been on well-preserved surface hoar in sheltered areas at treeline and below. While this persistent weak layer has been difficult to find near the ski area, riders continue to find it throughout the forecast region. The mid and lower snowpack are well settled.

Mountain Weather Report


ISSUED: Monday March 2, 2026 11:00 MST

SYNOPSIS: Mainly sunny/clear to partly cloudy skies and unseasonably mild temperatures are expected as a flat upper ridge of high pressure remains over southern BC today and tonight. Sunny periods should persist early Tuesday. A frontal system will track inland from the south coast for increasing cloud and increased wind Tuesday afternoon and occasional snow/rain (questionable snow level) Tuesday night.

FREEZING LEVEL: Rising to between 1800 & 2000 metres this afternoon. A freezing layer should develop at surface tonight. Between 1700 & 2000 metres Tuesday.

FERNIE ALPINE RESORT FORECAST (1615 metres):

TODAY: Mainly sunny. High 4 or 5.

TONIGHT: Clear with a few bands of cloud or cloudy periods. Low -1 or -2.

TUESDAY: Sunny with cloudy periods during the morning, increasing cloud and increasing wind during the afternoon. Slight chance of light rain or wet flurries late in the day. High 3 or 4.

TUESDAY NIGHT: Cloudy with occasional snow/rain. Snow level 1600-1800 metres, dropping to near 1300-1400 metres. Light accumulation. Windy at times. Low 0 or -1.

OUTLOOK:

WEDNESDAY: Cloudy with a few flurries, possible afternoon and evening snow showers. Minor to light accumulation. Gusty winds. High 1 or 2. Low near -4.

THURSDAY: Cloudy with sunny breaks. 40-60% chance of afternoon and evening flurries. High 0 or 1. Low near -5.

FRIDAY: Cloudy with sunny breaks and a slight chance of flurries. Possibly windy at times. High 1 or 2. Low near -3.

CONFIDENCE/DISCUSSION: Fair to good confidence today through Tuesday, fair Tuesday night onward. There’s still some uncertainty of the local temperature with the current temperature inversion which could persist through Tuesday. The computer models are in relatively good agreement for occasional precipitation Tuesday night however the snow level is questionable initially during the evening. The snow level will trend lower as the system passes eastward overnight. An upper trough and its unstable airmass will follow the cold front Wednesday and Wednesday evening, the guidance is increasingly variable with the positioning of the trough and the possibility of local snow showers. Some computer models now confine the more significant snow showers south of Fernie. The guidance is still highly variable Thursday onward. A drier but unstable northwesterly flow should develop Thursday. A dirty ridge of high pressure is possible Friday.

 

Ron Lakeman.
