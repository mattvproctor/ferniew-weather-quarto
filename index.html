Problems

Small storm slabs are expected to remain reactive due to the underlying layer of large surface hoar and a crust. Use extra caution in sheltered areas at treeline, where triggering is likely.



Further Detail
Avalanche Summary

Numerous storm slabs were observed in the region on Saturday and Friday. Most of these avalanches occurred at treeline and below and failed on the late January surface hoar/crust layer described in the snowpack summary. They have been reported to have propagated widely (up to 300 m). While most of them were small (size 1 to 1.5), explosive controls near Fernie produced large slabs (up to 2.5).

Snowpack Summary

15 to 30 cm of snow now overlies the late January surface hoar/crust layer. This new snow has been accompanied by strong south and west winds, meaning that the crust is still on the surface on southerly aspects, while deeper deposits are found on north and east aspects. The late January surface hoar is largest on sheltered treeline and below treeline features from 1500 to 1900 meters and may not exist on exposed terrain in the alpine. The mid and lower snowpack is well settled.

Mountain Weather Report


ISSUED: Saturday January 31, 2025 11:15 MST

SYNOPSIS: An upper ridge of high pressure will briefly shift eastward across southeastern BC for partly cloudy skies during the majority of today. Increasing cloud is expected during the late afternoon or early evening and there’s also a 60% chance of light precipitation this evening as the leading edge (warm front) of a Pacific frontal system clips the area. Partial clearing is possible in its wake overnight and early Sunday. The trailing cold front and a following upper trough should provide increasing cloud by Sunday afternoon and occasional precipitation (dropping snow level) late Sunday and Sunday night.

FREEZING LEVEL: Rising to between 1800 & 2100 metres this afternoon. Dropping to between 1000 & 1300 metres overnight. Fluctuating between 1300 & 1700 metres Sunday.

FERNIE ALPINE RESORT FORECAST (1615 metres):

TODAY: Cloudy with sunny periods, increasing cloud late in the day. High near 3.

TONIGHT: Mainly cloudy with a 60% chance of light rain or wet flurries during the evening, snow level 1600-1800 metres. Cloudy with fog patches and possible clear periods overnight. Low -1 or -2.

SUNDAY: Possible morning fog patches and sunny breaks, otherwise cloudy. 60% chance of snow/rain late in the day. Snow level 1500-1700 metres. Gusty winds. High 2 or 3, possibly tending cooler late in the day.

SUNDAY NIGHT: Occasional snow or a few snow showers (dropping snow level) and gusty winds during the evening, light accumulation. Diminishing chance of flurries and possible clear breaks & fog patches overnight. Low near -2.

OUTLOOK:

MONDAY: Cloudy with sunny breaks and morning fog patches, slight chance of precipitation late in the day. High 1 or 2. 60% chance of precipitation (rising snow level) Monday night. Low near 1.

TUESDAY: Cloudy with sunny breaks and possible fog patches. High 2 or 3. Low near 0.

WEDNESDAY: Cloudy with sunny periods, possible fog patches. High near 3. Low near 0.

CONFIDENCE/DISCUSSION: Fair confidence. Some guidance confines the light precipitation associated with the approaching warm front just north of Fernie this evening. There’s also some uncertainty of the snow level this evening. A deepening temperature inversion could result in extensive low cloud/fog Sunday. Once again the snow level is questionable late Sunday afternoon, it should drop as the cold front passes during the evening. Airmass instability could result in variable snow showers Sunday evening. Another weaker Pacific disturbance is currently timed for late Monday-Monday night, the snow level could be higher. A strong upper ridge of high pressure should dominate Tuesday onward.

 

Ron Lakeman.
