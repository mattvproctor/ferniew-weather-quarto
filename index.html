Avalanche Bulletin

Updated on 2026-02-16 17:00:00 MST, valid until 2026-02-17 17:00:00 MST.

Valid for: Flathead-Lizard.

Summary
As storm snow accumulates, avalanche hazard will rise, creating vary dangerous avalanche conditions. Travel in avalanche terrain is not recommended.
  • Avoid avalanche terrain during periods of heavy loading from new snow, wind, or rain.
  • Storm slab size and sensitivity to triggering will likely increase through the day.
  • Use increased caution at all elevations. Storm snow is forming touchy slabs.
  • Storm slabs in motion may step down to deeply buried weak layers and result in very large avalanches.
  • Be aware of the potential for larger than expected storm slabs due to buried surface hoar.

Avalanche Summary

On Sunday, size 2 storm slab avalanches were artificially triggered in steep alpine terrain On Saturday, Widespread storm slab avalanche activity was observed. Slabs were size 1.5 to 2, triggered naturally and artificially. Going forward, we expect storm snow to remain reactive especially where it is being redistributed into lee terrain by moderate wind from the south.

Snowpack Summary

Storm snow totals could be 30 to 40 cm by end of day Tuesday. New storm snow overlies a crust on solar facing slopes from clear skies on Sunday and, wind affected snow on north facing terrain. Below this, a crust buried on the weekend is supportive on south facing and breakable on north facing terrain. On north aspects we continue to see reactivity on layers of crust, facets and/or surface hoar formed in late January and early February. The persistent problem remains a concern especially in less travelled areas.

Lakeman’s Lookout

The following snippet is taken from the Mountain Forecast section of FAR’s website. Bear in mind that it will be up to 24 hours old by the time you read it! Many thanks as always to Ron Lakeman for his hard work.

Mountain Weather Report


ISSUED: Monday February 16, 2025 11:00 MST

SYNOPSIS: Snow will develop this morning and should become heavier at times this afternoon and especially tonight and Tuesday morning as a substantial wave of moisture pushes northeastward in advance of a large upper trough of low pressure digging southward along the coast of BC. The snow should ease to a few flurries Tuesday afternoon. The upper trough should push slightly inland for variable cloud and a 50% chance of flurries Tuesday night. Increased wind and colder trending temperatures are also expected late Tuesday and more so Tuesday night as a modified Arctic airmass begins to gradually push west of Alberta.

FREEZING LEVEL: Possibly rising slightly above surface this afternoon and again Tuesday afternoon. Surface tonight. 

FERNIE ALPINE RESORT FORECAST (1615 metres):

TODAY: Periods of snow, possibly heavier during the afternoon. Accumulation from 5 to 10 cm. Increasing wind, more so at the upper elevations. High -2 or -3.

TONIGHT: Snow, heavy at times. Accumulation from 12 to 20 cm. Windy at times. Low -6 or -7.

TUESDAY: Snow, heavy at times during the morning, easing to a few flurries mid or late afternoon. Moderate to heavy accumulation, possible 12 to 22 cm. Increasing wind by late afternoon. High near -4, possibly trending colder late in the day.

TUESDAY NIGHT: Cloudy with clear breaks and a 50% chance of flurries. Windy. Low -16 or -17.

OUTLOOK:

WEDNESDAY: Cloudy with sunny breaks and a 60% chance of afternoon flurries. Minor to low end light accumulation possible. Windy. High near -13. 60% chance of flurries Wednesday night. Low near -19.

THURSDAY: Cloudy with sunny periods and slight chance of flurries. Possibly windy at times. High near -11. Low near -20.

FRIDAY: A mix of sun & cloud, slight chance of flurries. High near -9. Low near -17.

CONFIDENCE/DISCUSSION: Fair to good confidence of the general pattern, poor to fair confidence of the local details. The computer models are variable with the timing of the heavier snow however they all still suggest a heavy storm total snowfall. Some favour a lighter than predicted amount snow today and favour the late overnight and Tuesday morning hours for the heaviest rate of fall. The timing/positioning of the Arctic airmass is also questionable. The temperature profile suggests low density snow today-tonight and the possibility of Pacific moisture overrunning an Arctic airmass further complicates the snowfall prediction Tuesday. Airmass instability should produce a few flurries at times and is also a risk for snow showers (greater rate of fall) Tuesday night through Wednesday night or Thursday morning. Surface based high pressure of Arctic origin could dominate Thursday afternoon through Friday.

 

Ron Lakeman.
